## 3. The generalisation experiment (RQ3)

### 3.1 What is being asked

Kitsios et al. (ASE 2025) already established the headline: re-evaluated on **unseen**
functionality, task-specific models lose **up to 48%, on average 31%** F1, while LLMs lose ~3%.
That result is *not* the contribution here and must be cited, not re-discovered (`SCOPE.md` Part 5).
The question this phase answers is narrower and is the deck's third expected result:

> **Does the size of the drop differ across negative-selection strategies?**
> Δ = F1_seen − F1_unseen, per condition, as a mean over the three seeds **with the spread
> visible**. The condition set is now the full factorial: C1–C3 (supplies) and C4–C6 (the same
> supplies with the Phase-2 bouncer), so Phase 3 also asks whether the bouncer's Phase-2 rescue
> (§9.4: escape the degenerate mode) and its cost (−0.31 vs C1) **transfer to unseen
> functionality** — reading the C4-vs-C2 and C6-vs-C3 Δ pairs against the H1/H2 fork of §9.5.

If Δ is statistically indistinguishable across the trained conditions, that is a finding ("on this
corpus, with these labels, negative hardness does not change the unseen-functionality drop"), not a
failure.
No significance tests, p-values or multiple-comparison corrections — they are out of scope
(`SCOPE.md` Part 5); report means, spreads and the per-seed values.

### 3.2 Protocol (Route A: hold out whole functionalities)

- Hold out **k = `SPRIME_HELDOUT_K` = 3** functionalities; train on the remaining **20**.
- **"Best-represented" must be a measured rule, not an assumption** (D4). s′ is described as
  functionality-*balanced*, but the per-functionality pair counts have not been recorded in this
  repo. Print them, commit the counts, and state the selection rule (highest pair count, ties
  broken by functionality id) before looking at any F1.
- Report **F1_seen** (pairs whose functionality was trained on), **F1_unseen** (the three held-out
  functionalities) and **Δ**, per condition, per seed, then mean + spread across seeds 13/14/15.
- **C0 is evaluated once** on the same seen/unseen partition, exactly as in Phase 2 — same
  tokenizer, pooling, truncation and scoring path, no fine-tuning.
- The holdout is **the same three functionalities for every condition and every seed**. A different
  holdout per condition would confound strategy with difficulty.

### 3.3 Where the negatives come from — the one genuinely open design question (D3)

s′ is a **labelled pair** dataset (`code1, code2, label, functionality_id`). It has no anchor
stream and no mined negative pool, and Phase 2's conditions are defined by *how negatives are
mined from the CodeXGLUE train corpus*. "Train on s′" therefore needs an explicit choice:

| | What it does | Cost | What Δ then means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(A) Evaluate the Phase 2 checkpoints on s′** | no new training; score the eighteen existing models on seen/unseen | ~0 GPU-h | transfer of a CodeXGLUE-trained model, not training on s′ |
| **(B) Fine-tune on s′, mining negatives from s′ itself** — C1 random non-clone partner, C2 BM25-nearest non-clone, C3 cosine-nearest non-clone, plus **C4/C5/C6: the same three supplies with the Phase-2 bouncer** (clone/corpus exclusion → drop J ≥ `C4_JACCARD_MAX` → skip `C4_SKIP_HEAD` survivors), all inside the 20 held-in functionalities | one encode pass over s′'s fragments (≤ 9,200 texts) + 18 short runs | a few GPU-h | the intended reading: the same manipulation — including the denoising factor — on a corpus with functionality labels |
| **(C) Fine-tune on the Phase 2 triples, excluding held-out functionality** | — | — | **not possible**: CodeXGLUE fragments carry no functionality ids — recovering them is "the single largest unbudgeted task in the plan" (`FINAL_SPEC.md` §0.1), and `GROUND_TRUTH.md` puts it plainly: "you probably cannot recover functionality IDs for the CodeXGLUE fragments cheaply" |

**Recommended: (B)**, because it is the only option that keeps *negative strategy* as the
independent variable while training on functionality-labelled data — with (A) recorded as a
free secondary reading if the runs are cheap. (B) obliges entry criterion 4 (hardness check on the
s′ negatives) and a recorded `VERSION`-level note that the generalisation runs use a different
corpus from the main table, so the two tables are **not** directly comparable in absolute F1.

### 3.4 Budget

s′ is 4,600 pairs total, so the training cap must be **smaller** than the main 32,000 — the spec's
"six smaller runs" language is deliberate; **do not normalise the generalisation runs to the main
cap** (`CLOUD.md` §5.6). Record the chosen cap in `settings.py` next to `SPRIME_HELDOUT_K` and keep
it identical across conditions and seeds. At cap 16,000 the s′ runs are ≈ half a main run each
(≈ 20 min on the T4), so the full design — **6 conditions × 3 seeds = 18 runs** — is ≈ 6 GPU-h,
the same total the original 9-run plan budgeted. If quota runs out, the ladder is `SCOPE.md`
P6-8: cut **seeds** before cutting **conditions** (3 → 2 seeds before a condition disappears);
"complete weak beats incomplete strong".

### 3.5 Noise

A 3-functionality holdout out of 23 on 4,600 pairs will be noisy, and the audit already puts a
36% label-noise floor under every negative. Per `SCOPE.md` P6-6: **if the numbers are wildly
noisy, report the noise.** Do not add holdout folds until the variance goes away. Leave-one-
functionality-out rotation (mean and range of Δ across folds) is the first SHOULD if time allows
(`SCOPE.md` Part 3.2) — it is an explicit upgrade, not a rescue.
