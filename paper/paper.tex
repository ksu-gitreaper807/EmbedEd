\begin{abstract}
Contrastive fine-tuning of code encoders relies on mined hard negatives, yet in
pair-labelled code corpora a ``hard'' negative is frequently a false negative:
an unlabelled semantic clone. We present a controlled seven-condition study of
negative selection for Java clone detection with GraphCodeBERT and a triplet
objective: random negatives (C1), lexical hard negatives (C2, BM25), semantic
hard negatives (C3, embedding similarity), and each supply re-mined under a
simple similarity bouncer that discards negatives closer to the anchor than its
positive (C4--C6), plus the untuned encoder (C0). On the CodeXGLUE benchmark
split (415{,}416 test pairs, 13.7\% positive), semantic and lexical hard
negatives do not merely underperform random negatives --- they \emph{collapse}
to the all-negative base rate (C2 $0.248$, C3 $0.271$ vs.\ ceiling $0.241$),
with validation thresholds saturated at $1.0$: the models learn to predict
``non-clone'' for everything. A 50-pair blind audit attributes C3's supply to
30\% false negatives and locates the mechanism: collapse is governed not by the
false-negative \emph{rate} (C3 and the partly-rescued C4 both carry 30\%) but
by the false-negative \emph{surface} --- whether contaminating negatives are
simultaneously functional clones and near-duplicates --- with the triplet
margin acting as an amplifier. The bouncer restores roughly half the collapse
(C4 $0.435$) without rescuing it, and a Jaccard $\geq 0.40$ cut removes
$\sim$40\% of C2's measured contamination while discarding no audited clean
pair. On an independently collected corpus with functionality labels
(s$\phi$, 4{,}600 pairs, 23 functionalities), the ordering replicates across
rulers, and the comparison gains a floor the imbalanced benchmark hides: the
untuned encoder scores $0.693$ on unseen functionality, and \emph{no}
poisoned-mining condition beats doing nothing at all (C2 $0.667$, C4
$0.669$; C3 ties it with a saturated scorer), while clean training yields the
only gains (C1 $0.703$, C5 $0.724$). We conclude with a measurement-first
protocol --- audit the negatives, report the positive rate, and read
saturated thresholds as a failure signature --- and with the experiments
(denoising ladder, FN-weighted objectives) that our mechanism account
predicts will matter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Contrastive fine-tuning has become the default way to adapt pretrained code
encoders such as CodeBERT~\cite{feng2020codebert} and
GraphCodeBERT~\cite{guo2021graphcodebert} to clone
detection~\cite{lu2021codeglue}. Beyond the in-batch positives, practitioners
mine \emph{hard negatives}: corpus snippets that are lexically or
semantically close to an anchor but labelled non-clone. The intuition ---
harder negatives teach a finer-grained decision boundary --- descends from
metric learning~\cite{schroff2015facenet,hermans2017triplet}. In
pair-labelled code corpora, however, the label supply is contaminated at the
source: BigCloneBench, the standard corpus behind CodeXGLUE's clone task,
carries incomplete and partly erroneous labels, especially among the
weakly-typed clones that semantic miners retrieve
preferentially~\cite{svajlenko2015bigclonebench,krinke2022harmful,krinke2025misuse}.
A hard negative mined from such a corpus is frequently a \emph{false
negative}: a genuine semantic clone labelled as a non-clone. Training then
pushes apart code that is genuinely equivalent.

This paper reports a controlled, seven-condition study of that failure mode
and its partial repair, in Java clone detection. We hold the encoder, loss,
hyperparameters, and seeds fixed and vary \emph{only} how training negatives
are selected; a pre-registered hardness gate verifies that the manipulation
takes effect before any F1 is interpreted; blind audits measure how much
false-negative contamination each supply actually carries; and an independent
functionality-labelled corpus lets us ask the generalisation question on a
second ruler. The conditions form the full factorial of \{random, BM25,
semantic\} supplies $\times$ \{with, without\} a \emph{bouncer}: a
per-triple filter that discards any mined negative closer to the anchor than
the anchor's positive --- the RocketQA~\cite{qu2021rocketqa} /
NV-Retriever~\cite{moreira2024nvretriever} positive-aware cut, applied as a
hard similarity threshold, here Jaccard $\geq 0.40$.

\finding{Research questions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (benchmark):} How does negative-selection strategy change
benchmark clone-detection performance?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (mechanism):} Why do poisoned supplies collapse, and what
exactly does the bouncer change?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (unseen functionality):} Does the strategy change how much
performance drops on functionality never seen in training?
\end{itemize}

\finding{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A seven-condition controlled study (three supplies $\times$ bouncer
$\times$ untuned baseline) with pre-registered manipulation gates, fixed
seeds, and per-run configuration fingerprints (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
\item The collapse finding: on the benchmark, mined-lexical and mined-semantic
supplies collapse to the all-negative base rate with saturated thresholds ---
and the failure mode sits \emph{below} what the benchmark's imbalance makes
visible (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).
\item A mechanism decomposition supported by blind audits: the
false-negative \emph{surface} (functional clone $\wedge$ near-duplicate),
not the false-negative \emph{rate}, determines collapse; rate is the dose,
the triplet margin is the amplifier, and the bouncer's partial rescue is
explained quantitatively (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).
\item Cross-corpus replication with a new floor: on unseen functionality
every poisoned condition performs at or below the \emph{untuned} encoder,
while the strategy ordering and the saturation signature replicate
(Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}).
\item A measurement-first protocol for negative-selection studies in
label-contaminated domains, and the experiments our account predicts will
matter next (Sections~\ref{sec:protocol-lessons} and~\ref{sec:future}).
\end{enumerate}

All artifacts (per-run checkpoints, predictions, audit sheets, mining
summaries, and the configuration fingerprints that make each number
reproducible) are committed to a public snapshot referenced in the appendix.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Clone detection and its benchmark}
BigCloneBench~\cite{svajlenko2015bigclonebench} annotates Java method pairs
by functionality (true clones) and otherwise labels non-clones; CodeXGLUE's
clone-detection task~\cite{lu2021codeglue} derives a binary pair
classification benchmark from it, on which fine-tuned CodeBERT-family models
report F1 around $0.95$ under the benchmark's own
convention~\cite{lu2021codeglue}. That convention evaluates on the natural
pair prevalence (13.7\% positives in our split), which caps an
all-negative scorer at F1 $\approx 0.24$ --- a floor we make explicit
throughout, because two of our findings live near it.

\subsection{The ground truth is contested}
Krinke~\cite{krinke2022harmful} documented that BigCloneBench's labels are
incomplete across functionalities and that a substantial share of Weak
Type-3/Type-4 judgements are subjective or erroneous ($\geq$15\%). A
follow-up statistical audit~\cite{krinke2025misuse} sampled 406 WT3/T4 pairs
and found 93\% mislabelled for \emph{semantic} similarity, and showed that
the CodeXGLUE clone subset inherited the flaw. The practical consequence for
us: the harder a miner works to find \emph{semantically similar but
labelled-different} code, the more it selects exactly the region where the
labels are wrong. Related work in code search reached the same conclusion
from the loss side: false negatives among ``negative'' pairs should be
weighted rather than trusted~\cite{li2023rethinking}.

\subsection{Hard negatives help --- until they are false}
Dense-retrieval and metric-learning results frame both expectations. DPR
found random, BM25, and gold negatives nearly equivalent without in-batch
reuse~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr}; RocketQA measured that $\sim$70\% of
top-retrieved ``negatives'' in MS-MARCO are actually positives, and that
denoising --- not harder mining --- is what pays~\cite{qu2021rocketqa};
NV-Retriever operationalised positive-aware mining (filter negatives closer
than the positive)~\cite{moreira2024nvretriever}; debiased contrastive
objectives correct the false-negative bias analytically under sampling
assumptions~\cite{chuang2020debiased}; and fair-comparison audits find
mining gains often marginal once everything else is
controlled~\cite{musgrave2020reality}. In code search specifically,
CoCoHaNeRe reports strong retrieval gains from differentiable hard-negative
mining~\cite{fan2025cocohanere}, while Soft-InfoNCE's authors show gains
come from \emph{re-weighting} possibly-false negatives, not from hardness
itself~\cite{li2023rethinking}. Clone detection differs from all of these in
one decisive way: its corpus labels are functionality-derived, so the
false-negative contamination of mined negatives is structural, not
incidental.

\subsection{Generalisation of clone detectors}
Kitsios et al.~\cite{kitsios2025unseen} released s$\phi$ (4{,}600 pairs over
23 functionalities) and showed that state-of-the-art neural clone detectors
--- near-perfect on the benchmark --- drop by up to 48\% F1 (31\% on
average) when the pair's functionality was absent from training. No prior
study, to our knowledge, has asked whether the \emph{negative-selection
strategy} changes that drop; that is our RQ3, and the answer is one of the
paper's two surprises.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Corpora, model, and training recipe}
\textbf{Benchmark.} CodeXGLUE clone detection (BigCloneBench-derived,
Java): 9{,}126 fragments (8{,}063 unique texts), 901{,}028 training pairs,
415{,}416 test pairs, 13.68\% positive test prevalence
(all-negative F1 ceiling $0.2406$).
\textbf{s$\phi$}~\cite{kitsios2025unseen}: 4{,}600 pairs over 23
functionalities (a sampled variant of BigCloneBench with functionality
labels retained, \texttt{bcb\_v2\_sampled\_bf}); we hold out whole
functionalities \{10, 13, 14\} (the three highest-pair-count
functionalities, ties broken by id, fixed before any F1 exists) and split
the remainder into seen-validation and seen-training slices.
\textbf{Model.} \texttt{graphcodebert-base}~\cite{guo2021graphcodebert},
mean pooling, max length 512, batch 8, one epoch, AdamW (LR $2{\times}
10^{-5}$), triplet loss with margin $0.10$, fp16 autocast,
up to 32{,}000 training triples (benchmark) / a 16{,}000 cap (s$\phi$).
\textbf{Seeds.} 13/14/15 everywhere; threshold and evaluation policy frozen:
the decision threshold maximises F1 on the \emph{seen validation} slice only
and is applied unchanged to test --- we additionally report the threshold
itself, because its saturation turns out to be a failure signature.
\textbf{Metrics.} F1 (with precision/recall), MAP@R as the
threshold-free robustness check~\cite{musgrave2020reality}, and per-seed
spread; $n{=}3$ seeds per condition, so we report means $\pm$ sd and never
significance tests.

\subsection{Conditions: three supplies $\times$ the bouncer}
Negatives are mined once per corpus with anchors and positives fixed across
conditions; only the selection strategy varies (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}).
The naming is by build order, not by quality.

\subsection{Manipulation gates and audits}
Two pre-registered checks precede any interpretation. \textbf{G1 (hardness
gates):} the mined negatives must be measurably harder under the intended
supply, as a standardised gap in negative similarity, $d = 0.614$ on the
benchmark and $d = 0.886$ on s$\phi$, with the registered ordering
random $<$ lexical $\leq$ semantic; a manipulation that does not take effect
produces no evidence. \textbf{G2 (blind audits):} for each mined condition,
a stratified sample of 50 mined negatives is judged \emph{blind} to
condition (``is this negative actually a functional clone of the
anchor?''), sheet committed before unblinding. Audited false-negative rates
are reported with Wilson intervals. Two audits (C5/C6 on s$\phi$-adjacent
supplies) are registered but not yet unblinded
(\pending{audit\_c5, audit\_c6}; predictions: 0--2\% and 25--35\%
respectively); no C5/C6 claim in this paper relies on their outcome.

\finding{RQ1: mined hard negatives collapse below the random baseline, to
the base rate itself.} Both poisoned supplies do not merely lose to random
negatives --- they land \emph{on} the all-negative ceiling (C2 $0.2478$,
C3 $0.2707$, ceiling $0.2406$, untuned C0 $0.2572$), with 10 of 12 runs per
condition choosing a validation threshold of $1.0$: the model assigns
near-zero clone probability to everything, and the loss stops complaining
because the false negatives it is asked to push apart are, at inference
time, indistinguishable from ``everything''. The threshold-free MAP@R
confirms the ordering is not a thresholding artefact (C3 $0.183$ vs.\ C1
$0.663$). To our knowledge this is the cleanest demonstration in code
clone detection that \emph{harder} negatives can be strictly worse than
random ones --- not marginally, but catastrophically --- and the saturation
signature is the tell that separates ``no signal learned'' from ``signal
erased''.

\finding{The bouncer halves the collapse but cannot finish the job.} C4
(BM25 + bouncer) recovers to $0.4349$ --- roughly half the distance from
the collapsed cluster to the clean band --- with every threshold unpinned
and MAP@R $2.7\times$ C3's. The supply audit explains both the rescue and
its ceiling: C4 carries \emph{the same audited FN rate as C3} (30\%) but a
different residual composition (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). C5
(random + bouncer) confirms the bouncer is not itself a performance
intervention: $0.7399 \pm 0.0140$, statistically inside the C1 band. C6
(semantic + bouncer) lands between the clusters ($0.4722$, unpinned
thresholds), predicted by its supply measurement (13.6\% residual
contamination, Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).

\finding{The published-number tension, resolved.} Fine-tuned CodeBERT
reports $\approx 0.95$ F1 on this task under the benchmark convention. Our
clean-supply C1 reaches $0.7464$. The gap is a pair-regime difference, not a
harness defect: our evaluation uses the corpus-wide natural prevalence
(13.7\% positives, ceiling $0.24$), under which an all-negative scorer
scores $0.24$ and the published near-clone-heavy regimes do not apply. We
report the reconciliation rather than chase the $0.95$; MAP@R
($0.663$) provides the prevalence-robust companion.

\section{RQ2: mechanism --- rate is the dose, surface is the trigger}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The audits were designed to discriminate between two explanations: (a)
collapse is a \emph{dose} phenomenon (more false negatives, worse the
model), and (b) collapse depends on \emph{which} false negatives are
sampled. The data decide for (b), with (a) as a secondary effect:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C3 and C4 carry the same audited FN rate (30\%) yet sit
$0.16$ F1 apart with opposite scorer states} (C3: saturated, base-rate F1;
C4: unpinned, mid-recovery). Rate alone does not determine collapse.
\item \textbf{The discriminating variable is the FN surface.} Coding each
audited contaminant by two properties --- functional-clone-ness and
near-duplicate-ness (Jaccard $\geq 0.40$) --- the trigger configuration is
a negative that is \emph{both}. The bouncer removes precisely the
near-duplicate half of C3's contamination (it cuts everything at Jaccard
$0.40$), which is why C4 escapes saturation without escaping the FN rate:
it keeps the functional-but-not-near-duplicate contaminants, whose gradient
disagreement is resolvable rather than degenerate.
\item \textbf{The margin is the amplifier.} With triplet margin $0.10$, a
contaminant closer than the positive incurs a persistent positive loss
even at large absolute similarity: the optimum under such pressure is to
erase the representation differences entirely --- the degenerate solution
the saturation signature records. This links the collapse to the
false-negative bias known analytically from debiased contrastive
learning~\cite{chuang2020debiased} and to the ``hardest-negative'' gradient-noise
regime documented by the metric-learning reality check~\cite{musgrave2020reality}.
\item \textbf{Supply measurements predict condition outcomes.} Re-mined
under the bouncer, the residual candidates-contamination measured 0.27\%
for C5 (audit prediction: $\approx$0) and 13.6\% for C6 (prediction band
10--25\%); the semantic supply carries $\sim$31\% more lexical poison than
BM25's (10.5\%) --- a RocketQA-style measurement~\cite{qu2021rocketqa}
transplanted to code. The Jaccard cut removes $\sim$40\% of C2's
contamination in-sample while discarding \emph{no} audited clean pair
(clean maximum Jaccard $0.345$ vs.\ clone median $0.418$): the cut is not
free of false-negative risk in general, but on this corpus its operating
point sits in a genuine gap.
\end{itemize}

On the functionality-labelled corpus the absolute level rises (balanced

\finding{The ordering and the saturation signature replicate across
rulers.} C1 and C5 form the top band on both corpora; C2/C4 sit at the
bottom on both; C3's benchmark saturation reappears as s$\phi$ validation
thresholds pinned at $0.9998$--$0.9999$ with $\Delta \approx 0$ and unseen
F1 ($0.7057$) indistinguishable from the untuned encoder. Two corpora, two
prevalences, one ordering, one failure signature.

\finding{No poisoned-mining condition beats doing nothing.} The untuned
encoder transfers to s$\phi$ at $0.6934$ unseen F1. C2 ($0.6674$) and C4
($0.6687$) finish \emph{below} that floor; C6 ($0.6865$) ties it; C3
($0.7057$) matches it while exhibiting the saturated-scorer pathology ---
i.e., its apparent parity is the degenerate optimum wearing a benign
disguise. Only the clean supply (C1 $0.7031$) and its bounced control (C5
$0.7244$) convert fine-tuning into gains on unseen functionality. For
practitioners the operational summary is blunt: on functionality-labelled
code, mining your hard negatives from label-contaminated similarity
neighbourhoods is worse than not fine-tuning at all.

\finding{The drop itself is small; the prior literature's is not.}
Kitsios et al.\ report task-specific F1 drops up to 48\% (mean 31\%)
for benchmark-champion models~\cite{kitsios2025unseen}; our drops are
$\leq 0.054$. We attribute the difference to regime: triplet training on
$\leq$32k pairs with a small margin barely specialises the encoder, so
there is little specialisation to lose --- consistent with the transfer
reading, where even \emph{benchmark-trained} checkpoints score $0.63$--$0.75$
on s$\phi$ unseen pairs (secondary observation; C3's transfer rows are
again saturated). The RQ3 result is therefore about the \emph{strategy
ordering and the floor}, not about the size of the generalisation gap.

\section{A measurement-first protocol for negative-selection studies}
\label{sec:protocol-lessons}

Our failures and recoveries generalise into five cheap practices, each of
which earned its place by catching a real error in this project:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Report the positive rate and the all-negative ceiling next to
every benchmark F1.} Two of our conditions ``achieved'' F1 scores that are
the ceiling to two decimals; without the ceiling printed, collapse is
invisible.
\item \textbf{Report the chosen threshold, and read saturation as a failure
signature.} Thresholds pinned at $1.0$ identified the degenerate optimum on
both corpora, before any other instrument did.
\item \textbf{Audit the negatives blind, and gate headline claims on the
audit.} The 50-pair instrument converted ``we believe contamination
explains this'' into measured rates with intervals --- and falsified the
rate-only explanation.
\item \textbf{Gate the manipulation before the outcome.} The pre-registered
hardness gates ($d = 0.614$ / $0.886$, ordering random $<$ lexical
$\leq$ semantic) are what make ``strategy'' a treatment rather than a
hope.
\item \textbf{Keep a threshold-free companion metric.} MAP@R tracked every
F1 ordering here and cannot be gamed by an all-negative scorer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Written before the pending audits unblind, per the project's pre-registration
discipline.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Label noise bounds interpretation.} The benchmark's ground
truth is contested (93\% of audited WT3/T4 pairs mislabelled for semantic
similarity~\cite{krinke2025misuse}; $\geq$15\% subjective or
erroneous~\cite{krinke2022harmful}). Our test F1s measure ``agreement with
BigCloneBench labels'', not real-world semantic clone detection; the
mined-hard region is precisely where that agreement is worst.
\item \textbf{Scale and seeds.} One encoder family, one language, one loss
(triplet, margin $0.10$), three seeds per condition, no significance
tests; we report spreads. The collapse deltas ($\sim$0.45 F1) are far
outside seed noise ($\leq$0.03), but the poisoned-cluster orderings
(e.g., C6 vs.\ C4) are within it.
\item \textbf{One bouncer operating point.} The Jaccard $0.40$ cut is a
single, hard threshold chosen a priori; Section~\ref{sec:future}'s ladder
is the honest version of that axis.
\item \textbf{s$\phi$ inherits BigCloneBench.} The generalisation corpus
derives from the same universe; its independence is of labels
(functionality) and sampling, not of provenance.
\item \textbf{Pending instruments.} \pending{audit\_c5 / audit\_c6
contamination audits; nine C2/C3 benchmark re-evaluations (recorded
deltas $\leq +0.027$, no boundary crossing).} No claim here relies on
them; their outcomes can only refine Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}'s
supply bookkeeping.
\item \textbf{The bouncer is not tuned and its false-negative risk is
corpus-dependent.} The measured gap (clean max $0.345 <$ cut $0.40 <$
clone median $0.418$) is a property of this corpus, not a law.
\end{itemize}

\section{Threats to validity}
\textbf{Internal.} Seeds, data splits, thresholds, and hardness gates are
frozen before evaluation; per-run configurations are content-fingerprinted,
and every artifact path in the snapshot is hash-addressed. The main
residual threat is judge drift in the blind audits; sheets were committed
before unblinding and judgements are recorded per-pair with reasons.
\textbf{External.} Java + BigCloneBench + GraphCodeBERT only; the
mechanism account (surface over rate) predicts replication under any
label-contaminated similarity-mining regime, and dense-retrieval evidence
~\cite{qu2021rocketqa,moreira2024nvretriever} points the same direction,
but we have not run a second language or encoder.
\textbf{Construct.} ``Collapse'' is defined operationally (base-rate F1
with saturated thresholds), not metaphorically; ``false negative'' is
operationalised by blind human judgement on the audited samples.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

Ordered by how directly our mechanism account predicts their outcome:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Complete the registered audits} (C5/C6 contamination). Under
the surface account, C6's residual composition --- not its rate --- decides
whether semantic-supply rescue needs a different cut than Jaccard.
\item \textbf{The denoising ladder.} Replace the binary bouncer with a
continuous margin-relative cut, $\delta \in \{0.00, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$
(discard negatives with $\cos(a,n) > \cos(a,p) - \delta$), three seeds, C3's
supply. This traces the dose--response curve between C3 and C4 and turns
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} from a two-point observation into a curve. It
is the single highest-value next experiment.
\item \textbf{FN-aware objectives on the same supplies.} Soft-InfoNCE-style
negative re-weighting~\cite{li2023rethinking} and debiased
contrastive~\cite{chuang2020debiased} variants, trained on C2/C3's exact
supplies: if the surface account is right, re-weighting should recover
part of what the bouncer removes, without discarding hard examples.
\item \textbf{Margin as the amplifier, tested.} The same supplies at
margins $\{0.05, 0.10, 0.20\}$ --- the account predicts saturation onset
moves with margin at fixed contamination.
\item \textbf{Robustness of the ordering.} The winning condition on a
second encoder; the full factorial on one more language; and a
leave-one-functionality-out rotation on s$\phi$ to shrink the $\Delta$
error bars ($10\times$ the compute; a revision-stage item).
\item \textbf{Benchmark repair rather than avoidance.} Functionality labels
for the CodeXGLUE fragments (the s$\phi$ provenance makes a subset
recoverable), enabling contamination-aware negative sampling at benchmark
scale.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
On the standard benchmark, mined hard negatives for contrastive clone
detection are not a tuning knob but a hazard: both lexical and semantic
supplies collapse training to the all-negative solution, below the random
baseline and at the measurement floor itself. The collapse is governed by
the \emph{surface} of the false negatives --- near-duplicate functional
clones pushed apart under a margin that will not forgive them --- not by
their rate, and a one-line positive-aware filter repairs half of the damage
at zero cost to clean pairs. On unseen functionality, the same strategies
that collapse on the benchmark fail a sterner test: they do not beat the
untuned encoder. The actionable findings are cheap: print the ceiling,
watch the thresholds, audit the negatives blind, and gate the claims. Hard
negatives are only hard if they are true.
